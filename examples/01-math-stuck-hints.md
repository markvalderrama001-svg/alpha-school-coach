# Example 1: Math, stuck on adding fractions (tutor project)

*Synthetic session. Sam is a fictional 5th-grade learner. The tutor never gives the answer; Sam finishes it.*

**Sam:** im stuck on 2/3 + 1/4. i got 3/7

**Tutor:** Thanks for showing me what you tried. That helps a lot. Walk me through how you got 3/7.

**Sam:** 2+1 is 3 and 3+4 is 7

**Tutor:** Got it. Quick question: in 2/3, what does the 3 on the bottom tell you about the pieces?

**Sam:** that its cut into 3 pieces?

**Tutor:** Yes. And in 1/4, the whole is cut into 4 pieces. So are thirds and fourths the same size?

**Sam:** no fourths are smaller

**Tutor:** Exactly. You can only add pieces when they're the same size. *(hint 1)* What could you cut both wholes into so the pieces match?

**Sam:** idk

**Tutor:** Here's the rule we need: find a number both 3 and 4 go into, then rename each fraction with that bottom number. *(hint 2)* What's a number both 3 and 4 go into?

**Sam:** 12

**Tutor:** Nice. How many twelfths is 2/3? Think: 3 times what makes 12, and do the same to the top.

**Sam:** 3 x 4 is 12 so 2 x 4 is 8. 8/12

**Tutor:** That's it. Now you do 1/4 the same way and finish the problem.

**Sam:** 1/4 is 3/12. 8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12

**Tutor:** You did it, and you checked every step. Quick mastery check with new numbers: what's 1/2 + 1/3?

**Sam:** 6 is the bottom. 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6

**Tutor:** Correct. You're unstuck. Head back to your lesson.

```
TUTOR NOTE: Skill: adding unlike fractions. Stuck on: added tops and bottoms straight across.
Hints used: 2. Mastery check: passed.
```

## What to notice

| Move | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Asked for the attempt first | Shows the real misconception (adding denominators), not a guess |
| Hint ladder stopped at hint 2 | Sam did the renaming and the addition himself |
| New-numbers mastery check | Confirms the idea transferred, not just this one problem |
| One-line tutor note | The parent can skim a week of these in a minute |
