# Example 3: Weekly review and goal meeting prep (parent coach project)

*Synthetic session. The parent pastes the output of `py progress_log.py --demo` for Sam, a fictional 5th-grade learner, and asks for help with Sunday's goal meeting.*

**Parent:** Here's Sam's week. He hit his goal but something felt off on Tuesday. Prep the goal meeting.

**Coach:**

**Sam, week of Oct 19**

| Measure | Result | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Lessons mastered | 8 / 8 | Goal hit |
| Sessions | 4 / 5 | One missed night |
| Points | 67.5 | Under the 100 cap |
| Tutor used | 4 times | Fine, all on new skills |

| Skill | Mastery | Zone | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adding unlike fractions | 3 / 3 | stretch (77% avg) | Working hard here, that's healthy |
| Equivalent fractions | 3 / 3 | too easy? (95%) | Ready to move on |
| Main idea | 2 / 2 | target zone | Good |
| Making inferences | 0 / 1 | stretch (72%) | New skill, one try, no concern yet |

**What felt off on Tuesday:** one attempt at 60% in about 18 seconds per question. That's rushing, and it earned nothing, which is the system working. He retried at 85% and passed.

**Flags**

- Rushing once (Tuesday)
- Nothing else. No gap pattern, no avoidance.

**One adjustment:** keep the goal at 8. Name the rushing calmly. Don't raise the bar the same week you correct a habit.

**Goal meeting prep**


```
WEEK OF: Oct 19   LEARNER: Sam
1. Last week: lessons mastered 8 / goal 8 | sessions 4 / planned 5
2. Win to name: "Tuesday's fraction lesson didn't go well, and you came back and passed it at 85%. That's the skill."
3. Ask: "On Tuesday the first try went really fast. What was going on?"
4. This week's goal: let him propose. Coach range 8 to 9 lessons.
5. Reward earned: 67.5 points. Pay in person at the meeting.
6. Strategy change: read the explanation before answering on new skills.
7. My support: sit nearby for the first fraction lesson of the week.
```

**Script if he gets defensive about Tuesday**

> "You're not in trouble. Fast tries just don't earn anything, so they waste your time. Slow and right gets you done sooner."
