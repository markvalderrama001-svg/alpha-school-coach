# Example 2: Reading, finding the main idea (tutor project)

*Synthetic session. Sam is a fictional 5th-grade learner. The passage was written for this example.*

> **Passage (synthetic):** Honeybees do a "waggle dance" when they return to the hive. The direction of the dance points other bees toward flowers. The length of the waggle tells them how far away the flowers are. Scientists think this dance helps the whole hive find food faster.

**Sam:** the question says whats the main idea. i put "bees dance"

**Tutor:** Good start, you found the topic. Main idea is a bit more: what's the big point the author wants you to know *about* the dance?

**Sam:** that they dance when they come back

**Tutor:** That's one detail. Try this: read the last sentence again. What does the author say the dance *does*? *(hint 1)*

**Sam:** helps the hive find food faster

**Tutor:** Yes. Here's a trick: the main idea is the sentence that all the other sentences support. *(hint 2)* Do the sentences about direction and distance support "bees dance" or "the dance helps find food"?

**Sam:** the food one. direction and distance tell them where food is

**Tutor:** Exactly. Now write the main idea in your own words, one sentence.

**Sam:** Honeybees use a dance to tell other bees where to find food.

**Tutor:** That's a strong main idea, and you backed it up with the details. Mastery check: in this sentence group, what's the main idea?

> *Owls hunt at night. Their big eyes collect a lot of light. Their soft feathers let them fly almost silently.*

**Sam:** owls have body parts that help them hunt at night

**Tutor:** Correct. Back to your lesson.

```
TUTOR NOTE: Skill: main idea. Stuck on: gave the topic instead of the main idea.
Hints used: 2. Mastery check: passed.
```

## What to notice

- The tutor never wrote the main idea sentence. Sam did.
- Hint 2 gave a reusable strategy ("the sentence all the others support"), not the answer.
- The mastery check used a new text, so the skill had to transfer.
